Overview

Overwatch is a free-to-play first-person hero shooter, released in 2016. It is a team-based PvP game, where two teams of five match against each other, fighting against varying objectives. Play as one of over fifty unique heroes as you fight on maps from around the globe!

Roles

In Overwatch, every hero is categorized into a role, which reflects their general characteristics and abilities, as well as their purpose on the team.

Tank

Tank heroes lead the team in every engagement. Their goal is to create space for their team and take map control, opening up opportunities for the damage and support heroes to make plays. Tanks are sorted into one of three sub-roles based on their play style:

  • Bruiser: Focused on facing the enemy team directly, with short-range lethality and abilities that allow them to soak incoming damage. These are typically referred to in the community as brawl tanks.
  • Initiator: Possess great mobility and burst damage. Their goal is to take down valuable single targets with their team. These are typically referred to as dive tanks.
  • Stalwart: The most varied of the tank sub-role, these tanks do not fit into a specific archetype. These tanks are generally great at holding and controlling space, and most posses the ability to create barriers to protect their team.

Damage

Damage heroes are responsible for seeking out and eliminating enemies. Their primary goal is to use their abilities to apply pressure and secure kills. How they do that is based on one of four sub-roles:

  • Flanker: Mainly focus on melting low-health targets and distracting the enemy. They usually contain high movement abilities, allowing them to move around the map with ease. Flankers are either part of the Brawl archetype or the Dive archetype.
  • Recon: Heroes that are focused on keeping their distance from the enemy and gaining information about the enemy team. Recon is currently primarily character with flight abilities. Recon heroes fit into many archetypes, but are typically played in Dive or Poke team compositions.
  • Sharpshooter: Long-range and highly lethal, these heroes are focused on holding angles with their high headshot damage. Their attacks are usually hitscan and are part of the Poke archetype, though they can work in any team composition.
  • Specialist: A broad role, Specialists have highly-varied hero kits. Some are jacks of all trades, while some have strong area control. They mainly belong to the Brawl and Poke archetypes.

Support

Support heroes are the backbone of any team. They empower their allies, through healing and utility abilities. While some supports have high healing potential, that is not a requirement, instead containing higher utility potential. Supports are split into one of three sub-roles:

  • Medic: Medic supports provide a large amount of healing to their allies. While they contain some utility, their main usefulness comes from healing and sometimes damage. They do not belong to any specific team archetype.
  • Survivor: These supports possess strong movement abilities, but tend to be very squishy. Their abilities allow for them to engage or disengage from fights easily. Similar to medics, they can fit into multiple archetypes, such as Dive, Poke, and Brawl.
  • Tactician: Tactician supports provide a balance between healing and additional support abilities. Depending on the hero, they can debuff enemies or provide positive utilities for their teammates. These heroes are a key part of any team composition, as their unique blend of healing and utility are great boons for any team.

Game Modes

There are five core game modes for Quick and Competitive play. Each game mode is objective-based, with its own set of rules depending on the type.

Control

The Control game mode has two teams fight over a single capture point in a best-of-three format. Each team is trying to reach 100% capture percentage, at which point they will win the round. Each round is played on one of three options from the same parent map. Unlike all others, this game mode down not have a time limit. The Control maps are Nepal, Lijiang Tower, Ilios, Oasis, Busan, Antarctic Peninsula, and Samoa.

Escort

The Escort game mode has the attacking team escorting a payload to a delivery point, while the defending team attempts to prevent them until the timer runs out. The Escort maps are Watchpoint: Gibraltar, Junkertown, Rialto, Circuit Royal, Watchpoint: Grímsvötn, Dorado, Route 66, Havana, and Shambali Monastery.

Hybrid

Hybrid is a game mode that starts with a capture point that the attacking team must gain control over. Once that objective is taken, a payload is unlocked and must be delivered, similar to Escort. The Hybrid maps are King's Row, Numbani, Eichenwalde, Midtown, Paraíso, Hollywood, Blizzard World, and Neon Junction.

Push

In Push, two teams fight to gain control over a robot, escorting it as it pushes the objective to the end of the map. This mode is similar to Escort, except both teams are considered the attacking team, and they fight over a single objective that moves back and forth between the two map sides. The Push maps are New Queen Street, Colosseo, Esperança, and Runasapi.

Flashpoint

Flashpoint is a mode that is similar to Control, where teams are fighting over capture objectives. However, each map contains five points, with a new one unlocking after the previous point has been captured. Only one objective is available for capture at a time. The first team to capture three points wins the game. The Flashpoint maps are Suravasa, New Junk City, and Aatlis.
